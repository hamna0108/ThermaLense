## Dropped Methodologies & Conceptual Baselines (Decided by Evidence)

Approaches explored, empirically evaluated, and retired based on rigorous statistical and numerical criteria. Every number is traceable to active audit reports and result CSVs.

| Approach / Methodology | Reason Dropped / Classified | Replacement / Resolution | Status | Evidence File & Section |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **C-First Anomaly Cascade** | Naive C-first testing caused Class F recall to collapse to 32.1% on dev set, with 57.1% of faulty episodes falsely absorbed into Class C ($F \to C = 57.1\%$), and healthy false alarms on H rising to 8.6%. | Model-selection cascade: fits both F and C hypotheses and selects the lower BIC. Restores Class F recall to 79.7%, slashes $F \to C$ leakage to 5.2%, and reduces healthy FAR on H to 3.8%. | **Decided by evidence** | `data/audits/audit_anomaly.txt` (Section 3 lines 76–101), `data/results/anomaly_summary.csv` |
| **stuck_last_good Sensor Fault** | Dropped as a separate fault type because it duplicated `stuck_const` (identical freezing sensor reading behavior). | Consolidated into a single authentic stuck sensor type (sensor stuck at mid-range constant 0.5000) evaluated across 80 episodes per primary cell. | **Decided by evidence** | `data/audits/audit_anomaly.txt` (Section 4 lines 140–142), `data/raw/anomaly_raw.csv` |
| **Unanchored Joint-Estimation GLR Detector** | At high noise ($\sigma = 0.05$), estimating baseline $\alpha_0$ simultaneously from the jump window causes nuisance-parameter absorption (null fit shifts toward $\alpha_1$), dropping Class C recall on dev set to 12.5% ($N=10$) and 36.7% ($N=20$) vs Semi-Oracle power of 36.7% ($N=10$) and 57.1% ($N=20$). | Baseline-informed detector anchoring $\alpha_0$ with a separate healthy window; surges dev-set recall to 62.1% ($N=10$, +49.6% gain) and 69.6% ($N=20$, +32.9% gain). Fresh-test recall reaches 94.0% ($N=10, \sigma=0.01$), 51.0% ($N=10, \sigma=0.05$), 92.0% ($N=20, \sigma=0.01$), 59.0% ($N=20, \sigma=0.05$). | **Decided by evidence** | `data/audits/audit_anomaly.txt` (Section 3 lines 110–125 & Section 5 lines 180–201), `data/results/anomaly_summary.csv` |
| **Oracle-Grid GLR Testing** | Evaluating change-point GLR only at synthetic training grid times ($t_c \in \{0.1, 0.25, 0.5\}$, $m \in \{0.5, 2.0\}$) provides an artificial grid-matching advantage. | Continuous 2D GLR evaluated on off-grid change-points ($t_c \in \{0.15, 0.35, 0.70\}$, $m \in \{0.7, 1.5, 3.0\}$); outperforms rigid BIC tree by +8.3% to +16.1% delta across all 4 primary cells. | **Decided by evidence** | `src/study_anomaly.py` (lines 483–484 & 638–641), `data/audits/audit_anomaly.txt` (Section 6 lines 203–218) |
