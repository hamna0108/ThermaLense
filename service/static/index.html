OVERVIEW

ThermaLens

A thermal digital twin for plant engineers and operators to monitor heat dissipation, detect faulty temperature sensors, and simulate thermal profiles in real time.

Operational Tasks

Thermal Inversion

Estimate heat-spread

Estimate the rate of heat dissipation through a material from temperature sensor measurements.

What you enter: Sensor positions, timestamps, and observed temperature readings.
What you get: Estimated thermal diffusivity rate (α̂) with 95% confidence interval and fit verification.
Diagnostics & Health

Check sensors for faults

Detect whether temperature deviations are caused by genuine thermal changes or sensor hardware faults.

What you enter: A 20-reading healthy baseline followed by operational sensor measurements.
What you get: Automated status verdict (Healthy, Anomalous Heat, Sensor Fault, or Sudden Change).
Optimal Scheduling

Plan sensor timing

Optimize measurement timestamps to capture the most informative temperature changes with fewer sensors.

What you enter: Sensor budget, physical sensor position, noise tolerance, and time window.
What you get: An optimized two-stage measurement schedule concentrated near peak information.
1D Simulation

See the heat map

Simulate and visualize how heat propagates across a 1D thermal bar through space and time.

What you enter: Diffusivity rate, spatial resolution, and time snapshot to evaluate.
What you get: Interactive color heat map, cross-section temperature curve, and model comparison.
2D Surface Heat

2D steady-state heat sources

Calculate equilibrium temperature distributions across a 2D surface under distributed heaters.

What you enter: Heating pattern (harmonic wave frequencies or localized spot heaters) and grid size.
What you get: 2D temperature contour field, solver accuracy comparisons, and high-frequency safety alerts.

Recommended Sampling Schedule


          

            

              Click Compute Sampling Schedule to generate the recommended measurement times.
            

          


          

            
            

              How this works: Measuring heat spread accurately depends heavily on when you take readings. Instead of guessing or spacing them evenly, we divide your sensor readings into two phases: first, a few early readings spread across the test quickly establish a rough cooling rate. Then, based on that rough estimate, the remaining focused readings are concentrated right at the moment when the bar is cooling fastest and temperature changes are most sensitive to diffusivity. This two-stage approach sharpens your final estimate and produces narrower confidence intervals with the same total number of readings.

Split equally N₁ / N₂: The total reading budget N is divided into N₁ = ⌊N/2⌋ readings in Stage 1 and N₂ = N − N₁ readings in Stage 2.

                
Stage 1 (Exploration): Points are placed on a logarithmic grid between t_min and t_max to uniformly resolve unknown temporal scales across orders of magnitude.

                
Stage 2 (Concentration): Points sample the Fisher information sensitivity density w(t) = t² exp(−2 α π² t / L²), dynamically concentrating observations around the peak sensitivity point t* = L² / (π² α̂₁).

Heat Over Time

        
Pick a material and see how the temperature along a bar changes with time.

        

          Show details
          

            
Mathematical model: Spatio-Temporal Solution Field u(x, t) for 1D heat diffusion ∂u/∂t = α ∂²u/∂x² with ends held at Tedge and starting sinusoidal peak Tpeak.

            
Exact Fourier formula: u(x, t) = Tedge + (Tpeak − Tedge) · sin(πx/L) · exp(−α π² t / L²).

            
Converted normalised parameter: αnorm = α · T / L² = 0.130000.

            
Relative difference L₂: ||uAI − uSolver||₂ / ||uSolver||₂. Figures 4 and 5 in technical report.

          Show details
          

            Stage 2 · 2D Poisson −∇²u = f on [0, 1]² with homogeneous Dirichlet boundaries, exact sparse LU vs FNO vs linear ridge baseline.
          

        

      


      
      

        
Prominent Notice: High-Frequency Source Term Detected

        

          The specified source term contains harmonic wavenumbers exceeding m > 4 or n > 4. Empirical benchmark testing proves that FNO degrades severely on high-frequency out-of-distribution modes (median relative L₂ error spikes to 80.4%, max 229.4%), whereas the linear baseline maintains ~1.5% and the exact sparse direct solver maintains exact accuracy.
